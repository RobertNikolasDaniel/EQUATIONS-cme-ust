# 01 — Cash Treasury Bond Pricing

A cash Treasury bond is just a stream of promised cash flows.

The price is the present value of:
1. coupon payments
2. principal repayment at maturity

---

## 1. Basic Present Value

For any cash flow:

\[
PV = \frac{CF}{(1+r)^t}
\]

Where:

- \(PV\) = present value
- \(CF\) = future cash flow
- \(r\) = discount rate
- \(t\) = time in years

---

## 2. Price of a Coupon Bond

\[
P = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{C_i}{(1+y)^{t_i}} + \frac{F}{(1+y)^T}
\]

Where:

- \(P\) = bond price
- \(C_i\) = coupon cash flow at time \(t_i\)
- \(F\) = face value / par value
- \(y\) = yield to maturity
- \(t_i\) = time to coupon payment
- \(T\) = time to maturity
- \(N\) = number of remaining coupon payments

---

## 3. Treasury Coupon Amount

Treasury notes and bonds usually pay coupons semiannually.

\[
C = \frac{c \times F}{2}
\]

Where:

- \(C\) = semiannual coupon payment
- \(c\) = annual coupon rate
- \(F\) = face value

Example:

\[
c = 4.00\%, \quad F = 100
\]

\[
C = \frac{0.04 \times 100}{2} = 2
\]

So the bond pays \(2\) every six months.

---

## 4. Semiannual Bond Pricing

Because Treasury coupons are semiannual:

\[
P = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{C}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^i} + \frac{F}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^N}
\]

Where:

- \(y\) = annual yield
- \(y/2\) = semiannual yield
- \(N\) = number of semiannual periods

---

## 5. Expanded Coupon Bond Formula

\[
P =
\frac{C}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^1}
+
\frac{C}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^2}
+
...
+
\frac{C + F}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^N}
\]

Final payment includes both coupon and principal:

\[
C + F
\]

---

## 6. Closed-Form Bond Price

If every coupon is equal:

\[
P = C \left( \frac{1 - (1+\frac{y}{2})^{-N}}{\frac{y}{2}} \right)
+ F(1+\frac{y}{2})^{-N}
\]

This is just:

\[
P = PV(\text{coupon annuity}) + PV(\text{principal})
\]

---

## 7. Discount Bond

A bond trades below par when:

\[
P < F
\]

This usually means:

\[
y > c
\]

The market yield is higher than the coupon rate.

---

## 8. Premium Bond

A bond trades above par when:

\[
P > F
\]

This usually means:

\[
y < c
\]

The market yield is lower than the coupon rate.

---

## 9. Par Bond

A bond trades near par when:

\[
P \approx F
\]

This usually means:

\[
y \approx c
\]

The market yield is close to the coupon rate.

---

## 10. Zero-Coupon Bond

For a zero-coupon bond:

\[
C = 0
\]

So the price is only:

\[
P = \frac{F}{(1+y)^T}
\]

For semiannual compounding:

\[
P = \frac{F}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^N}
\]

---

## 11. Yield From Price

There is usually no clean algebraic solution for yield on a coupon bond.

Instead, yield is the value of \(y\) that solves:

\[
P = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{C}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^i} + \frac{F}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^N}
\]

In practice, this is solved numerically.

---

## 12. Price-Yield Relationship

Bond prices and yields move inversely:

\[
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} < 0
\]

Meaning:

\[
y \uparrow \Rightarrow P \downarrow
\]

\[
y \downarrow \Rightarrow P \uparrow
\]

---

## 13. Duration Intuition

The price sensitivity to yield is approximately:

\[
\Delta P \approx -D_{mod} \times P \times \Delta y
\]

Where:

- \(D_{mod}\) = modified duration
- \(P\) = bond price
- \(\Delta y\) = change in yield

---

## 14. DV01

DV01 is the dollar value of a 1 basis point move in yield.

\[
DV01 = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta y_{bp}}
\]

For a 1 bp move:

\[
\Delta y = 0.0001
\]

Approximation:

\[
DV01 \approx D_{mod} \times P \times 0.0001
\]

---

## 15. Macaulay Duration

\[
D_{Mac} =
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{N} t_i \times \frac{CF_i}{(1+y)^t_i}
}{
P
}
\]

Where:

- \(D_{Mac}\) = weighted average time to receive cash flows
- \(CF_i\) = cash flow at time \(t_i\)
- \(P\) = bond price

---

## 16. Modified Duration

\[
D_{mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1+\frac{y}{m}}
\]

Where:

- \(m\) = compounding frequency
- for Treasuries, usually \(m = 2\)

So:

\[
D_{mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1+\frac{y}{2}}
\]

---

## 17. Convexity

Duration is a linear approximation.

Convexity adjusts for curvature:

\[
\Delta P \approx
-D_{mod} P \Delta y
+
\frac{1}{2} Cvx \times P \times (\Delta y)^2
\]

Where:

- \(Cvx\) = convexity
- \(\Delta y\) = yield change

---

## 18. Full Price Approximation

\[
\frac{\Delta P}{P}
\approx
-D_{mod}\Delta y
+
\frac{1}{2}Cvx(\Delta y)^2
\]

This is the basic duration-convexity approximation.

---

## 19. Cash Bond Pricing Summary

The core cash bond price is:

\[
P = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{CF_i}{(1+y_i)^{t_i}}
\]

For a plain Treasury bond with one yield:

\[
P = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{C}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^i}
+
\frac{F}{(1+\frac{y}{2})^N}
\]

Or closed form:

\[
P = C \left( \frac{1 - (1+\frac{y}{2})^{-N}}{\frac{y}{2}} \right)
+ F(1+\frac{y}{2})^{-N}
\]

That is the whole game:

\[
\text{Bond Price} = PV(\text{Coupons}) + PV(\text{Principal})
\]
