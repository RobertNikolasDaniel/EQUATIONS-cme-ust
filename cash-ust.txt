# CASH UST EQUATIONS

Simple visual reference sheet for core U.S. Treasury cash bond math.

---

# 1. PRESENT VALUE

Future cash flows discounted back to today.

\[
PV = \frac{CF}{(1+r)^t}
\]

Where:

- \(CF\) = future cash flow
- \(r\) = discount rate
- \(t\) = time

---

# 2. TREASURY COUPON PAYMENT

Treasuries pay semiannual coupons.

\[
C = \frac{c \times F}{2}
\]

Where:

- \(C\) = coupon payment
- \(c\) = annual coupon rate
- \(F\) = face value

Example:

4% coupon Treasury:

\[
C = \frac{0.04 \times 100}{2} = 2
\]

Pays:

- \$2 every 6 months
- \$100 principal at maturity

---

# 3. TREASURY DIRTY PRICE

Full bond value including accrued interest.

\[
P_{dirty}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\frac{C}{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^i}
+
\frac{F}{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^N}
\]

Interpretation:

Bond price is:

- present value of coupons
- plus present value of principal

discounted using market yield.

---

# 4. TREASURY CLEAN PRICE

Quoted market price excluding accrued interest.

\[
P_{clean}=P_{dirty}-AI
\]

Where:

- \(AI\) = accrued interest

Most Treasury screens quote clean price.

---

# 5. ACCRUED INTEREST

Interest earned since last coupon payment.

\[
AI=C\times\frac{d}{D}
\]

Where:

- \(d\) = days since last coupon
- \(D\) = days in coupon period

---

# 6. PRICE ↔ YIELD RELATIONSHIP

Bond prices and yields move inversely.

\[
y\uparrow \Rightarrow P\downarrow
\]

\[
y\downarrow \Rightarrow P\uparrow
\]

Higher yields mean heavier discounting.

---

# 7. YIELD TO MATURITY

The yield that solves:

\[
P_{dirty}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\frac{CF_i}
{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^i}
\]

Yield is usually solved numerically.

There is normally no closed-form solution.

---

# 8. MACAULAY DURATION

Weighted average time of bond cash flows.

\[
D_{Mac}
=
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
t_i
\left(
\frac{CF_i}
{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^i}
\right)
}
{P}
\]

Interpretation:

Longer duration = more rate sensitivity.

---

# 9. MODIFIED DURATION

Approximate price sensitivity to yield changes.

\[
D_{mod}
=
\frac{D_{Mac}}
{1+\frac{y}{2}}
\]

This is the important trading duration.

---

# 10. PRICE CHANGE APPROXIMATION

First-order duration approximation.

\[
\Delta P
\approx
-D_{mod}P\Delta y
\]

Interpretation:

- yields up → price down
- yields down → price up

---

# 11. DV01

Dollar value of a 1bp move in yield.

\[
DV01
\approx
D_{mod}\times P\times0.0001
\]

Interpretation:

Approximate dollar PnL for a 1bp rate move.

---

# 12. CONVEXITY

Second-order curvature adjustment.

\[
\frac{\Delta P}{P}
\approx
-D_{mod}\Delta y
+
\frac{1}{2}Convexity(\Delta y)^2
\]

Duration alone becomes inaccurate on large moves.

Convexity corrects the curve.

---

# 13. ZERO COUPON TREASURY

No coupon payments.

Only discounted principal.

\[
P
=
\frac{F}
{(1+y)^T}
\]

---

# 14. PREMIUM BOND

Bond trades above par.

\[
P > F
\]

Usually:

\[
c > y
\]

Coupon exceeds market yield.

---

# 15. DISCOUNT BOND

Bond trades below par.

\[
P < F
\]

Usually:

\[
y > c
\]

Market yield exceeds coupon.

---

# 16. PAR BOND

Bond trades near face value.

\[
P \approx F
\]

Usually:

\[
y \approx c
\]

---

# 17. CORE TREASURY IDENTITY

\[
Bond\ Price
=
PV(Coupons)
+
PV(Principal)
\]

That is the entire foundation of cash UST pricing.
